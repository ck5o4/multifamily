# Baker Trails — IC memo, 2026-08-17 (OM received)

Supersedes IC_MEMO_2026-08-02.md, which was built on an ESTIMATED rent roll.
Source: Baker_Trails_OM_June.pdf, attached by Justen Rider 2026-08-13.

**Verdict: PASS.** Not on price alone — on the shape of the risk.

## What the OM changed

The old estimated roll assumed 9x 2BR @ $800 + 3x 3BR @ $900, all twelve
occupied. Every Baker number before today rested on that. The OM says
otherwise, and the differences run in both directions.

Rent roll rebuilt at deal-intake/baker-trails/rentroll_baker_trails_OM_2026-08-13.csv.
It ties to the OM exactly: in-place $6,650/mo, pro forma $10,500/mo.

Economic vacancy against pro forma today is **36.7%**.

## Two facts that decide it

**1984 construction.** Every prior Baker run printed "vintage capex is INACTIVE
and the P10 is optimistic" because no year_built was passed. It is now. A
42-year-old building with no T-12 and no invoices for the 2020-21 work means
unknown remaining life on roofs, HVAC, water heaters and everything behind the
walls. That is what fattens the left tail.

**Four long-term vacancies.** The OM's own framing — ownership "has not been
aggressive with leasing" — is the charitable reading. The unkind one is that
these units do not lease at $800-875 in this submarket. If they were easy
fills, they would be full. The buyer carries that risk, not the seller.

## The numbers

Deterministic ladder (location='baker', taxes reassessed per price):

| Target | Stabilized @7.5% vac | Lease-up blend @20% vac |
|---|---|---|
| 13% | $582,000 | $423,000 |
| 16% | $541,000 | $393,000 |
| 22% | $471,000 | $342,000 |

Note the stabilized 13% price ($582K) is HIGHER than the old $528K figure —
the real pro forma rents are better. That is the trap. It only holds if the
lease-up happens.

Monte Carlo, vintage capex active, n=2000 — this is what settles it:

| Price | Vacancy | P(IRR>=13%) | P10 | P50 | Beats index |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $582,000 | 7.5% | 31% | -23.9% | 5.6% | **39%** |
| $528,000 | 7.5% | 38% | -27.7% | 8.4% | **46%** |
| $528,000 | 20% | 18% | -26.6% | -1.4% | **24%** |
| $450,000 | 7.5% | 51% | -24.6% | 13.3% | **57%** |
| $423,000 | 20% | 27% | -40.4% | 1.8% | **34%** |

## Why this is a pass, not a lowball

House rule: beat the index or pass. At $528,000 — the number quoted to Rider
before the OM was read — this beats the index **46% of the time**. That is a
coin flip, which is an automatic pass. Even at $423,000, a 44% discount the
seller will never take, the realistic lease-up case beats the index only 34%
of the time. The P10 sits between -24% and -40% across every scenario: a real
chance of a substantial loss, driven by the 1984 capex hazard.

There is no price in the plausible negotiating range that fixes a fat left
tail. Discounting buys expected return; it does not buy documentation.

## What would reopen it

1. A T-12, or six months of bank statements showing real collections/expenses.
2. Verifiable capex documentation — contractor names and dates beat nothing.
3. Signed leases on one or two of the vacant units at pro forma, proving the
   lease-up thesis.

Most of what is being priced right now is uncertainty, not the property. Retire
the $530K Crexi price alert: a price cut alone no longer revives this deal,
because price was never the binding problem.
