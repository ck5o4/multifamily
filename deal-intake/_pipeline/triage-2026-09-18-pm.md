No model run is published here, deliberately. The apartment expense stack in
pymodel defaults does not describe a mobile home court, and with lot rent, home

What can be said without inventing anything:

- **A quoted 6.00% cap is below the debt constant.** At 6.75% on a 25-year
  amortization the annual constant is ~8.29%. Buying a 6.00% cap with 75% debt is
  negative leverage on day one — cash-on-cash lands below the cap rate, not above
  it. At the quoted cap this does not reach the 13% band on in-place income.
- Therefore the case, if there is one, has to come from **embedded value, not the
  quoted yield**: vacant utilities-live pads (the CLAUDE.md screening order #1), or
  dying homes on good pads to replace at turnover (#2), or lot rent materially
  below the Livingston market. A full park at a true 6% cap is a pass.
- Brokers quote cap on the seller's own expense numbers. Eden's own P&L reset its
  cap materially once real insurance and management were loaded in. Expect the same
  here.

**Verdict: PULL THE PRICE.** Asking price, pad count vs occupied pads, vacant
live-pad count, who owns the homes, and lot rent. Those five fields decide it;
everything else is noise until they land.
